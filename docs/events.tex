\section{How to read this document}

This document describes every event currently registered by the game, including
events that are disabled and actions initiated by the player.  It follows the
implemented rules rather than comments or labels that may have become outdated.

Let \(t\) be the current quarter; \(i_t\), \(\pi_t\), \(u_t\), and \(R_t\)
denote the latest nominal interest rate, inflation rate, unemployment rate, and
Central Bank reputation.  Rates are measured in percentage points.  Let
\(N_E(k)\) count occurrences of event \(E\) in the preceding \(k\) recorded
quarters, and let \(L\) be the number of consecutive most-recent quarters in
which the interest rate was below 1 percent.

An impact schedule \sched{x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_7} gives the additive shock in the
event quarter and the following seven quarters.  Omitted variables have zero
impact throughout.  Inflation, unemployment, the natural unemployment rate,
and the equilibrium real interest rate (\(r^*\)) are in percentage points.
``Demand'' is an output-gap shock in percentage points.

The source event calibrations specify unemployment effects, but the live model
converts each such schedule into demand shocks using Okun's law:
\[
  \text{demand shock}_j=-\frac{\text{calibrated unemployment effect}_j}{\beta_u},
  \qquad \beta_u=0.7\text{ by default}.
\]
The tables therefore report both the direct demand schedule used by the model
and, in parentheses, its calibrated unemployment equivalent.  The ultimate
unemployment outcome also depends on the rest of the macroeconomic model, so
the parenthetical schedule is not a guarantee of the observed path.

\subsection{Selection procedure}

In ordinary random selection, the game proceeds as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item It evaluates each event's probability terms, adds them, and clamps the
        result to the interval \([0,1]\).
  \item It multiplies that result by the configured event-probability scale
        (normally 1), then clamps it again to \([0,1]\).
  \item Every eligible event receives an independent random draw.  If several
        pass, the game chooses one of those passing events uniformly.  Thus the
        formula below is an event's chance to enter the finalist set, not always
        its exact chance to become the one displayed event.
  \item At most one newly selected random event is announced per quarter, but
        effects already queued from earlier events continue and add together.
\end{enumerate}

Difficulty adds a global cooldown after a selected event: 20 quarters in
Principles, 10 in Senior, and zero in Central Banker mode.  Difficulty-specific
eligibility is noted below.  A sufficiently large negative ``repeat block''
forces the clamped probability to zero.

In Principles mode after the player-start boundary, a separate cooperative
fiscal rule runs before ordinary selection.  If no fiscal event occurred in the
last four quarters, deflation (\(\pi_t<0\)) together with unemployment above its
natural rate causes either Fiscal Deficit or Spending Wave to be selected with
equal probability.  If \(\pi_t>10\), Fiscal Surplus is selected with probability
50 percent.  These selections bypass the ordinary formulas and global cooldown.

\[
p=1\%+\min\!\left(\max\!\left(\frac{i_t-i_{t-3}}{100},0\right),5\%\right)

over three quarters and by up to 1.5 points during a sub-1-percent rate streak.

\section{Player-triggered policy events}

These actions are not random, so their firing probability is not a stochastic
percentage.  They occur when the player selects them and all availability rules
pass.  They are available only in Central Banker mode, and only one player action
may be initiated in a quarter.  Their effects can overlap previously queued
effects.

\event{Quantitative Easing}
\prob Player choice; no action-specific cooldown.  It may be selected in any
quarter in which no other player event has already been selected.

\impact Demand \sched{0.5,1,0.65,0.4,0.25,0.15,0.1,0.05}; inflation
\sched{0.05,0.1,0.065,0.04,0.025,0.015,0.01,0.005}.  While any part of this
schedule remains active, it also halves Major Financial Crisis risk following a
recent Financial Crisis.

\message Headline: ``Central Bank Launches Asset Purchases.''  Detail: ``The Central Bank has announced a new asset-purchase programme to support demand and ease financial conditions.''

\event{Announce Future High Rates}
\prob Player choice, provided reputation is strictly above 0.70 and at least four
quarters have elapsed since this same announcement was last used.

\impact Adds \sched{1,1,1,1} to interest-rate pressure.  This affects the demand
transmission mechanism; it does not directly overwrite the policy rate.

\message Headline: ``Central Bank Signals Higher Rates Ahead.''  Detail: ``The Central Bank has signalled that interest rates are likely to remain higher in the coming quarters.''

\event{Announce Future Low Rates}
\prob Player choice, provided at least four quarters have elapsed since this same
announcement was last used.

\impact Adds \sched{-1,-1,-1,-1} to interest-rate pressure.  This affects the
demand transmission mechanism; it does not directly overwrite the policy rate.

\message Headline: ``Central Bank Signals Lower Rates Ahead.''  Detail: ``The Central Bank has signalled that interest rates are likely to remain lower in the coming quarters.''

\section{Scenario-forced events}

Scenario setup can force existing events independently of their ordinary firing
probability.  These are not additional event definitions: they use the same
impacts and messages listed above.

During the pre-player history, the Depression scenario forces a Major Financial
Crisis three iterations before control passes to the player.  At the same point,
Stagflation forces one of Global Supply Shock, Pandemic Outbreak, or Natural
Disaster, weighted in proportion to their ordinary probabilities (0.8, 0.5, and
1 percent, respectively).  The High Inflation scenario forces Spending Wave if
one has not already occurred; it also multiplies that event's ordinary baseline
probability by ten during setup.  The Stable Economy scenario suppresses random
event selection for its final ten setup iterations.

Custom-scenario setup may also name and force any registered random or
informational event.  A forced event bypasses its probability formula, but uses
its normal message and complete queued impact schedule.
